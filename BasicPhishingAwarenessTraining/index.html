What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack where an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick victims into revealing sensitive information or performing unsafe actions.

Email Phishing

Fake emails designed to steal passwords or sensitive information.

Spear Phishing

A targeted phishing attack aimed at a specific person or organization.

Smishing

Phishing attacks delivered through SMS or messaging apps.

Vishing

Voice-based phishing attacks performed through phone calls.

How to Recognize a Phishing Email

  • Suspicious sender address
  • Urgent or threatening language
  • Requests for passwords or verification codes
  • Unexpected attachments
  • Suspicious links
  • Grammar and spelling mistakes
  • Fake account suspension warnings
  • Offers that seem too good to be true
From: support@paypa1-security.example

Subject: URGENT: Your Account Will Be Suspended

We detected unusual activity on your account. Verify your account immediately.

CLICK HERE TO VERIFY YOUR ACCOUNT

Red Flags

  • The sender domain imitates a real brand.
  • The email creates urgency and fear.
  • It requests immediate login verification.
  • The link may lead to a fake login page.

How to Recognize Fake Websites

Misspelled Domains

Example: paypa1.example instead of the legitimate domain.

Suspicious URLs

Attackers may use misleading subdomains or unrelated domains.

Unexpected Login Pages

Be cautious if a website unexpectedly asks you to sign in again.

Poor Design

Fake sites may contain broken links, spelling mistakes, or unusual layouts.

HTTPS alone does not guarantee that a website is trustworthy. Always verify the complete domain name.

Social Engineering Tactics

Urgency

"Your account expires in 15 minutes."

Fear

Attackers may claim that your account or money is at risk.

Authority

Attackers may pretend to be a manager, bank employee, or IT administrator.

Trust

Attackers may impersonate coworkers, friends, or trusted companies.

Real-World Style Examples

Fake Microsoft Login

An employee receives an email claiming that their Microsoft password has expired. The email links to a fake login page designed to steal credentials.

Fake Bank Alert

A victim receives an SMS claiming suspicious bank activity and is sent to a fake banking website.

CEO Fraud

An attacker impersonates a company executive and requests an urgent money transfer or gift-card purchase.

Best Practices to Avoid Phishing

  • Check the full sender email address.
  • Inspect links before clicking.
  • Do not open unexpected attachments.
  • Never share passwords or verification codes.
  • Use multi-factor authentication.
  • Use unique passwords.
  • Visit important websites directly.
  • Verify unusual requests using another communication channel.
  • Keep software updated.
  • Report suspicious emails.
